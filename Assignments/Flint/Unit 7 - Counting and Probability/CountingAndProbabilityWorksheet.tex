\section{Systematic Counting and Fundamental Counting Principle}

\begin{enumerate}

\item A restaurant offers 4 different appetizers, 6 different main courses, and 3 different desserts. How many different three-course meals can be ordered (one item from each category)?

\item A license plate consists of 2 letters followed by 3 digits. How many different license plates can be made if repetition is allowed?

\item A school is assigning ID numbers that consist of 1 letter followed by 4 digits. How many different ID numbers are possible?

\item A clothing store stocks shirts in 5 colors, pants in 4 sizes, and shoes in 6 styles. How many different outfits can be created by selecting one shirt, one pair of pants, and one pair of shoes?

\item A password must consist of 2 uppercase letters followed by 3 digits. How many different passwords are possible if repetition is allowed?

\item At a sandwich shop, customers choose: bread (5 options), cheese (3 options), meat (4 options), and vegetables (6 options). How many different sandwiches can be made?

\item A survey has 5 yes/no questions. How many different ways can the survey be completed?

\item How many different outfits can be made with 3 hats, 4 shirts, and 5 pairs of pants?

\item A restaurant has 3 types of soda, 2 types of juice, and 4 types of water. How many different beverage choices are available?

\item A code consists of one color (from 8 available), followed by one number (from 0-9), followed by one letter (from 26 available). How many different codes are possible?

\end{enumerate}

\section{Permutations}

\begin{enumerate}
\setcounter{enumi}{10}

\item In how many ways can 5 students be arranged in a line?

\item A coach is choosing a captain, co-captain, and treasurer from a team of 8 players. How many ways can these three positions be filled?

\item How many different 4-letter arrangements can be made from the letters A, B, C, and D (using each letter exactly once)?

\item In how many ways can 6 people be seated in 6 chairs around a conference table?

\item A competition has 10 participants. In how many ways can first, second, and third place be awarded?

\item A playlist has 7 different songs. How many different ways can the songs be arranged?

\item How many different 3-digit codes can be made from the digits 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, and 6 (without repetition)?

\item A teacher is arranging 4 students in a row for a photo. How many different arrangements are possible?

\item A company needs to select a president, vice-president, and secretary from 9 employees. In how many ways can this be done?

\item How many 5-letter arrangements can be made from the letters in the word WORLD (using each letter at most once)?

\end{enumerate}

\section{Combinations}

\begin{enumerate}
\setcounter{enumi}{20}

\item A committee of 3 people is being chosen from a group of 8 people. How many different committees can be formed?

\item How many ways can 4 books be selected from a shelf of 10 books?

\item In a lottery, 6 numbers are chosen from 1 to 49. How many different lottery tickets are possible?

\item A student must choose 2 elective classes from 5 available electives. How many different combinations of electives are possible?

\item How many different 5-card hands can be dealt from a standard 52-card deck?

\item A team is choosing 4 starters from a roster of 12 players. How many ways can the starting team be selected?

\item How many ways can 3 toppings be selected from 8 available pizza toppings?

\item A store is selecting 6 products from a catalog of 20 products to feature in an advertisement. How many different selections are possible?

\item How many ways can a person choose 2 vegetables from a list of 7 vegetables?

\item A committee needs to select 5 members from a group of 15 volunteers. How many different committees are possible?

\end{enumerate}

\section{Distinguishing Between Permutations and Combinations}

\begin{enumerate}
\setcounter{enumi}{30}

\item Is the following a permutation or combination problem? ``How many ways can 3 students be chosen to represent the class?''

\item Is the following a permutation or combination problem? ``In how many ways can 4 people be arranged in a line?''

\item A student is choosing 5 books from 8 books to take on a trip. Is this a permutation or combination? How many ways can this be done?

\item A principal is selecting a class president, vice-president, and secretary from 20 students. Is this a permutation or combination? How many ways can this be done?

\item In how many ways can a coach select 6 players from 14 for a game? Is this a permutation or combination?

\item Is it a permutation or combination to arrange 7 distinct paintings on a wall?

\end{enumerate}

\section{Basic Probability}

\begin{enumerate}
\setcounter{enumi}{36}

\item A fair die is rolled. What is the probability of rolling a 4?

\item A bag contains 5 red marbles and 3 blue marbles. If one marble is drawn at random, what is the probability that it is red?

\item A spinner is divided into 8 equal sections numbered 1 through 8. What is the probability of spinning a number less than 5?

\item A standard deck of cards has 52 cards. What is the probability of drawing a heart?

\item A coin is flipped. What is the probability of getting tails?

\item A box contains 4 green, 6 yellow, and 5 red balls. If one ball is randomly selected, what is the probability that it is yellow?

\item A number is randomly selected from 1 to 20. What is the probability that it is a multiple of 3?

\item A fair die is rolled. What is the probability of rolling a number greater than 4?

\item A bag contains 8 candies: 3 chocolate and 5 vanilla. What is the probability of randomly selecting a vanilla candy?

\item A spinner has 10 equal sections: 4 are blue, 3 are red, and 3 are green. What is the probability of spinning blue?

\end{enumerate}

\section{Independent Events and Dependent Events}

\begin{enumerate}
\setcounter{enumi}{46}

\item A fair coin is flipped twice. What is the probability of getting heads both times? (These are independent events.)

\item A bag contains 4 red marbles and 6 blue marbles. If two marbles are drawn with replacement, what is the probability that both are red?

\item Two cards are drawn from a standard deck without replacement. What is the probability that both are hearts?

\item A die is rolled, and then a coin is flipped. What is the probability of rolling a 3 and flipping heads?

\item A bag contains 3 green and 5 yellow balls. If two balls are drawn without replacement, what is the probability that both are yellow?

\end{enumerate}
